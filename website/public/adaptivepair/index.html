The opportunity

Ask questions with purpose.

When survey time is scarce, choosing the next comparison can matter as much as how responses are scored. The goal is to help researchers make defensible decisions—not just produce a neat ranking.

◎

Find useful comparisons

Estimate where additional votes could reduce uncertainty, while maintaining exploration and coverage safeguards.

≋

Respect score differences

Study preference probabilities and score gaps, not simply item order. Avoid assuming that raw win rates remain comparable under adaptive sampling.

◈

Make it easy to evaluate

A standalone reference implementation and testable simulations let a small product team assess the idea without a production integration.

Reproducible research

Show the evidence. Show the limitations.

Initial simulation study: 8 items, 80 votes per survey, 40 repetitions per strategy and scenario. Figures show mean absolute error in estimated pairwise win probabilities. Lower is better.

Standard preferences0.09360.08870.0888

Exploratory synthetic results, not a customer pilot, product integration, or statistically established improvement. The standard scenario slightly favors balanced selection; all methods perform worse with cyclic preferences. Adaptive sampling may change unadjusted win-rate scores.

Customer-first validation

What we're working to prove.

Before anyone adopts adaptive matching, it needs to show that meaningful customer outcomes improve—not just that the optimizer selects interesting pairs.

01 / MEASUREMENT

Preserve meaningful gaps

Compare adjusted estimates, raw win rates, and held-out observations under adaptive versus balanced sampling.

02 / REALISTIC SURVEYS

Stress-test respondent behavior

Model multiple preference segments, skips, fatigue, attrition, unequal participation, and uncertainty.

03 / LOW-FRICTION ADOPTION

Document the integration path

Offer a small next-pair interface, explicit fairness safeguards, reproducible tests, and no dependency on a hosted service.
